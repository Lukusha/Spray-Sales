# Single-product storefront design research

Reviewed 29 October 2026. There is no verified, comparable leaderboard in the sources reviewed establishing the world's highest-selling or highest-converting single-product website. Public sales claims, vendor case studies, ad-channel conversion changes and whole-store conversion rates are different metrics. The large brands below now sell multiple products; they are useful examples of businesses built around a recognizable core product, not all literal one-SKU shops today.

## Primary sources and limits

| Reference | Public evidence | Relevant design observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Squatty Potty](https://www.squattypotty.com/) and [its Simple stool page](https://www.squattypotty.com/products/simple-toilet-stool) | The brand reports over 8 million stools sold. This is company-reported sales volume, not a verified site conversion rate. | Large product imagery, price and purchase control together, product specifics, explanation in steps, and shipping/returns information. |
| [The Oodie](https://theoodie.com/) | The brand reports over 9 million Oodie Originals sold and over 4 million customers. Its current site has a broader catalog. | Strong, distinct branded sections, a recognizable core product, clear shopping actions and reassurance about delivery/returns. |
| [Flaus](https://goflaus.com/) | Its homepage reports over 450,000 users. No comparable store conversion rate was verified. | One dominant product story, benefit-led headline, repeated direct shopping CTA, product explanation and FAQ. |
| [Shopify's Hismile case study](https://www.shopify.com/uk/case-studies/hismile) | Shopify reports $300 million annual revenue in 2023 and 500% year-on-year global online sales growth. This is platform-reported, historical and not a single-product conversion-rate ranking. | Reliable checkout, localized currency/experience and a clear offer matter. Bundles were important for Hismile, but no unapproved discount bundles were added here. |
| [Oddit case studies](https://oddit.co/pages/case-studies) | The agency lists Oodie with a '3 to 5%' conversion figure. The page is vendor-reported and does not establish a globally comparable ranking or prove which individual visual choice caused a lift. | Useful directional evidence for testing simpler product communication; not grounds to promise this store the same result. |

## Applied to Crystal Care

- Product image beside a concise, explicit product category, benefit headline, offer, quantity and black purchase button.
- White shopping area, navy information sections and a black footer. Brand colors: white `#FFFFFF`, black `#111111`, navy `#0B1F3A`. Pale navy tints and neutral text are supporting tones, not extra brand colors.
- Plain, centered button labels; no interface arrow glyphs in the storefront, dialogs or merchant inbox. The manufacturer's product-label artwork is preserved.
- Immediate facts near the offer: 100ml, pump format and cash on delivery.
- Three-part reassurance strip, numbered product details, instructions, FAQ and repeated purchase action.
- Existing mobile purchase bar and guest checkout retained.
- No copied testimonials, fabricated sales counts, invented guarantees, unsupported before/after evidence or artificial countdowns.

Visual resemblance is a design choice, not evidence of equivalent conversion. The next meaningful measurement is this store's own completed/confirmed orders per relevant session, segmented by traffic source and device. For cash on delivery, monitor phone-confirmed orders, deliveries and refusals as well as form submissions.
